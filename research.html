Research

From sequencing reads to evolutionary history

The lab works across the full genomics workflow: assembling reference genomes, describing what those genomes express, and comparing them across species to understand how they came to be the way they are.

Genome assembly

Whole-genome sequencing and assembly

Chromosome-scale assembly from PacBio HiFi and Hi-C data, scaffolding, pseudomolecule construction and assembly quality assessment. The group has contributed to reference assemblies for mango, pigeonpea and jute, and to pseudomolecule development for Indian rice varieties.

  • HiFi and Hi-C assembly pipelines for heterozygous plant genomes
  • Pseudomolecule construction and genetic-map anchoring
  • Repeat masking, structural annotation and gene prediction
  • Organelle genome assembly and annotation
Transcriptomics

Transcriptome profiling and pathway analysis

RNA-seq experiment design, de novo and reference-guided assembly, differential expression and pathway reconstruction. Past work covers flowering-gene expression across developmental stages in mango and host response to root-knot nematode infection in tuberose.

  • De novo and reference-guided transcriptome assembly
  • Differential expression across tissues and developmental stages
  • Pathway reconstruction for secondary metabolite biosynthesis
  • Marker discovery from transcriptome data (SSR, miRNA)
Comparative genomics

Comparative and evolutionary genomics

Genome-wide comparison of gene families and repeat elements across species, and reconstruction of evolutionary relationships from conserved gene sets. This line of work grew out of the doctoral thesis on conserved genes and repeat elements in crop plants.

  • Annotation of intact LTR retrotransposons and transposable elements
  • Gene-family phylogenies across diverse eukaryotic species
  • Conserved-gene sets for deep phylogenetic reconstruction
  • Synteny and orthology between related crop genomes
Genotyping arrays

SNP discovery and genotyping array design

Variant discovery from resequencing data and the design of single-copy, genic SNP arrays for breeding programmes. Two arrays developed with collaborators are recognised technologies of the Indian Council of Agricultural Research.

  • 50K single-copy gene SNP chip for rice (OsSNPnks)
  • 62K genic-SNP array for pigeonpea (CcSNPnks)
  • 80K SNP genotyping chip for mango
  • Haplotype and association analysis in germplasm panels
Structural bioinformatics

Molecular modelling and protein–ligand interaction

Homology modelling, docking and motif characterisation, applied to herbicide tolerance in rice and to drug-target proteins in Leishmania and Bacillus anthracis.

  • Homology modelling and model validation
  • Protein–ligand docking and inhibitor optimisation
  • Motif characterisation in wild-type and mutant proteins
Genomic resources

Databases and genomic resources

Design and maintenance of web-accessible genomic databases for the research community, including MiSNPDb for mango phylogeography and varietal differentiation, and the Vanshanudhan rice gene database.

  • MiSNPDb — mango SNP and phylogeography resource
  • Vanshanudhan — rice gene database (HTML/JSP, MySQL)
  • Data delivery and reproducible analysis workflows

Collaboration

Where the work has been done

The lab's methods were built up through sustained collaboration with the National Institute for Plant Biotechnology, New Delhi, where the mango, pigeonpea and jute genome programmes were based, and with the School of Plant Sciences at the University of Arizona, where the HiFi and Hi-C assembly work was carried out.

We welcome collaboration on genome and transcriptome projects in crop and medicinal plant species, particularly where a reference assembly does not yet exist. Enquiries from prospective students and research fellows are also welcome.
